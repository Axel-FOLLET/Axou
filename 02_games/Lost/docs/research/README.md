# Research

Reference studies behind the cat's movement, proportions, behaviour and style. Each paragraph gives the study's method, its key figures and what it changed in the game. The values applied are those of the date of the study: the code is the reference for current values.

**Status of the figures.** "Measured" means measured by the study on images or files. "Literature" means a published source. "By eye" means an estimate on images, with the stated margin. No video in these studies has an attested real-time speed, so ratios are safer than absolute durations. Most ages are unknown. The target was a young adult cat; the game's hero is a kitten with young-adult proportions, by decision of 2026-10-22.

## Movement

**Real cats, frame by frame (2026-10-22).** Ten YouTube videos (720p at most) read through timestamped contact sheets; paw contacts extracted by kymogram on one side-view video at 24 fps. Measured on that walk: cycle 2.04 s (the note considers it probably slowed or slow), stance 1.25 to 1.50 s, duty factor 0.61 to 0.73, lateral sequence, fore foot following the same-side hind foot at 0.14 and 0.16 of the cycle. Read on contact sheets: sit to loaf ≈ 2.5 s with forepaw steps; descent to lying ≈ 1.5 s; roll on the back ≈ 0.4 s, then still for ≈ 19 s with no paddling; one lick every ≈ 0.45-0.5 s (n = 4); stretch ≈ 2.2 s (extend ≈ 0.3-0.5 s, hold ≈ 1.3 s, return ≈ 0.3-0.5 s); wake-up with the head ≈ 0.7 s, yawn ≈ 1.3 s, no standing. By eye (± 10° at least): hind leg 25-30° forward at contact and 30-40° back at lift. The clips redone on 2026-10-23 follow these figures: `WALK_DUTY` 0.67, front paw 0.15 cycle after the hind, a longer `Lie`, no paddling in `Pet`, one lick every 0.5 s and a new stretch (`tools/anim_cat.py`). Not measured: trot, joint angles.

**The cat of *Stray* (2026-10-19 and 2026-10-22).** A first study read about 25 written sources and 5 videos frame by frame, without sound, to understand why players attach to this cat. Written sources describe hand animation, a studio of about 28 people, live cats as models, footsteps recorded on many surfaces and a purr sent to the controller. Praised points: purposeless gestures, fragility, resting poses. Frame by frame: transitions of 0.5 to 1.5 s, holds of 4 to 6 s, tail low in the walk and wrapped when sitting or lying. A second study (6 videos, precision of one sampling step, ±1 step) measured: walk cycle ≈ 0.8 s (≈ 1.25 cycle/s), run ≈ 0.40 s per stride (≈ 2.5 strides/s), sit to sphinx ≈ 0.47 / 0.4 / 0.4 / 0.2 s (≈ 1.6 s total), stretch ≈ 2.6 s as a whole, jumps ≈ 0.25 to 0.4 s. Trailer and captured footage, so in-game speed is not guaranteed. This study led to the 0.8 s walk cycle (`WALK_RATE` 1.25), the two-stage lying down, idle activities and the list of improvements of 2026-10-20: ground contact, three-stage petting, tail by state, lively holds and a synthesised purr. The project never extracted anything from the game: the decision of 2026-10-19 limits the study to public videos and images.

## Proportions

**Published measurements (2026-10-22).** Veterinary publications read in full or in abstract, with no measurement by the study. Main sources: Poradowski 2024 (n = 37), Kartal 2026 (n = 55), Tollin & Koka 2009, Fleming 2020, Sieslack 2021. Published: withers 25.98 ± 2.57 cm, head width 62 mm, eye globe 19.75 ± 1.59 mm. Derived from these means: hind limb bones ≈ 12 % longer than fore (intermembral index 89.83 ± 3.22); pupil spacing 0.544 of head width; outer pinna 0.781 of head width; facial length 0.387 of skull length; head length 0.203 to 0.204 of head and body; tail 0.54 to 0.55 of head and body (known from a search summary only). The live muzzle length, ear spacing and iris diameter were not found.

**Faces measured on annotated photos (2026-10-22).** `tools/measure_cat.py` compares the model's head with medians from the CAT Dataset (Zhang, Sun, Tang, ECCV 2008): 9 997 heads with 9 points each, of which 6 836 are close to frontal. In inter-ocular distances (IOD): nose bottom 0.785 below the eyes, ear tips 1.589 above the eyes and 2.511 apart, ear height 0.983, base width 0.830, tilt 26.8°. The most stable measure is the eye position, at 0.672 of the height from the ear tips to the bottom of the nose (standard deviation 0.034). Limits: 2D projection, pitch not controlled, age and breed unknown. On 2026-10-23 `EAR_SCALE` 0.85 was retained, leaving the ears below the median on purpose because it looks cuter: tip height 1.487 against the 1.589 target.

## Behaviour

**Domestic cat behaviour (2026-10-22).** A literature review with no measurement. Young adult: 1 to 6 years. Contentment signals: half-closed eyes, ears forward, tail relaxed or raised and slightly curved (Nicholson 2021). The slow blink is studied by Humphrey 2020 (18 cats per experiment). Petting: positive on temples, ear bases and cheeks, negative at the base of the tail, belly to avoid (Haywood 2021: 535 observations of 5 minutes, agonistic behaviour 27 % in the control group against 16 % in the "CAT" group). Discomfort signals: head turned, ears flat, skin rippling on the back, nose lick, brief grooming, lashing tail. Time budget: rest and sleep 50 %, grooming 4 % (Eckstein & Hart 2000, 11 cats). No source measures the time before irritation. The note's deductions for the game are unverified: paddling only in a relaxed context, grooming from head to rear, tail raised for greeting, the rolled pose not inviting belly petting.

## Style

**Soft cartoon direction (2026-10-25, Tiny Glade section 2026-10-26).** A design note with choices, not measurements. Rules set in `src/style/palette.js`: `MeshToonMaterial` with 3 steps (0.5 / 0.78 / 1.0) and a hemisphere light filling the shadows, round shapes (mountains excepted, by later request), no post-processing, no global outline, `THREE.NeutralToneMapping` at exposure 1.0, a painted sky gradient and fog matching its horizon. Grass as tufts of 3 to 5 wide rounded leaves. The cat keeps `MeshStandardMaterial`, so its fur stays out of the toon treatment. From five frame-by-frame videos and one interview about *Tiny Glade*: cream and beige stone with rosy blocks, rounded blocks, several families of grass denser along walls, large rounded relief and atmospheric haze. Discarded: ray-marched global illumination, tilt-shift, straw and autumn tones.

## Petting

**Cat on its back (2026-10-25).** Four videos, 0.125 to 1.5 s sampling, by eye ± 20°. Standing to flank ≈ 0.75 s, the head leading by ≈ 0.4 s; flank to back head first, ≈ 0.5 s; hold nearly still for ≈ 16 to 18 s; rising head first, ≈ 1.5 to 1.75 s. On 3 of the 4 cats the chest is up and the pelvis stays on its side. Shoulders ≈ 120° to 150°, pelvis ≈ 80° to 100° (deduced). No forepaw paddling on any video. Literature: thoracic neutral zone up to 47° each side (Higurashi et al. 2026, 5 specimens and 2 live cats). The `Pet` clip was redone from this study. Petting moved to the flank on the same day, so on 2026-10-29 these references were found not to match the animation any more.

**Cat on its flank (2026-10-30).** Four YouTube videos, 0.125 s sampling, 11 contact sheets, angles by eye ± 20°. The head leads the trunk by ≈ 0.375 s to ≈ 1.25-1.5 s; trunk drop 0.25-0.375 s; head-up hold without support 3 to 5 s; forelegs forward, hind legs extended, trunk and legs still. Only the head answers the hand: chin raised 30 to 60°, top of the head or cheek pushed into the hand, eyes closed. Head settling is read on one video only. This note contains no code recommendation. It supported the `Pet` clip redone on 2026-10-30 (8.625 s) and its entries and exit in `src/cat/Brain.js` and `src/cat/Cat.js`.

## Carrying and setting down

**Carrying by the scruff (2026-10-29).** Ten photos read by eye (about ± 10 %), no video, no published source for the set-down. In a carry, the head is at back height or slightly below and the mouth at 0.6 to 0.7 × back height. A newborn forms a compact ball; older kittens hang with the rear brushing the ground. At set-down the neck drops 20 to 45° below horizontal and the kitten touches the ground while still held, then lies inert on its flank. Applied in the opening: `setHeadDip` with `HEAD_DIP` 1.3 rad, drop at contact (`dropAt` 0.01 m, measured gap 3.5 to 8.6 mm), `pause` 0.35 s, `dip` 0.9 s, `hold` 0.25 s, `rise` 0.6 s, `settle` 0.4 s, and a `Carried` clip with folded legs. The order "rear and flank first" is deduced from 3 stills, and the durations are proposals with no source.

## Sitting

**Seated non-player cats (2026-10-29 and 2026-10-30).** Twelve usable photos of seated adults compared with 10 game captures, by eye (± 5 % of the height from ground to skull top, ± 8° in profile). The real thigh top is at 44-50 % of the height against 29-33 % in the game; the belly opening 6-10 % against 23 %; thighs 1.4 × chest width against 1.18; forelegs leaning 5 to 20° against 0-3°; one convex back against a straight then hollow back. Changing the code parameters reached a limit (knee capped at 0.24-0.248 m), which led to a dedicated `SitNpc` clip with `SIT_NPC`, measured after as a belly opening of 7.9 / 7.2 % and a foreleg lean of 5.1°. The thigh top target was not reached. The seat was redone from new photos on 2026-11-01 (`SIT_NPC_V2`).

## Hugging

**Final hug (2026-10-29).** Thirteen photos retained of 20, by eye, no video. Bodies touch with no ground between them in 11 of 11 hugs. The kitten's head is under the chin or against the throat or chest in 6 cases. Maman's chin or cheek rests on the kitten's head and a foreleg lies across it in 3 cases. Body axes range from a 10-20° spoon to 60-90°. Kitten to mother length about 0.4 to 0.6. Applied: Maman's `Sleep` clip frozen at 3.0 s, lids at 0.6, front right paw on the kitten, breathing 1.1 rad/s and 0.035 rad, `HUG.hold` 7.0 s, `HUG.dist` 1.9, two purrs with their own settings. Measured in the game: head to mouth 0.0747 m horizontally (target ≤ 0.08). The purr values are judgement calls that no agent has heard, and no measurement of real purrs was read.

## Meows

**Recorded meows (2026-10-31, redone 2026-11-03).** No agent can hear, so the owner's complaint (frightening meows with a background noise) became nine thresholds fixed before processing: background under −55 dBFS, noise at least 45 dB under the meow, peak at −1.5 ± 0.3 dBFS, and pitch ranges (kitten ≥ 650 Hz, Maman 450-750 Hz, Chartiste ≤ 520 Hz, no transposition below × 0.9). All 8 files pass, with pitch after processing of 709 to 1044 Hz for the kitten, 549 and 601 Hz for Maman, 481 and 489 Hz for the Chartiste; margins are narrow on several. The sources were replaced with CC0 and public domain recordings ([credits](../credits.md)). The earlier Chartiste voice, transposed by × 0.72 and × 0.8, was probably the cause of its monstrous sound (hypothesis). The figures do not say whether the meows are cute: the owner listened on 2026-11-05 and accepted them. The Chagicien keeps a synthesised voice (`src/audio/Meow.js`).

## Walking in grass

**Cat walking in grass (2026-10-29).** Thirty-two Wikimedia Commons files read by eye, no pixel measurement and no video. Only 2 walks in tall grass, plus 2 uncertain ones, were found against a goal of 6. Stems stay upright before and behind the cat, which is seen through thin stems, and no flattened trail appears. Literature on the walk: 2.32 ± 0.32 km/h with a fore stance of 61.0 to 61.7 % (Mille et al. 2026, read as a summary); stance falling 40 to 50 % between 0.4 and 1.0 m/s (Harnie 2022). This study removed the trail and the lowered corridor around the cat. Applied in `src/world/Grass.js`, `Meadow.js` and `Flowers.js`: dithering so the cat shows through (`vSee`), a body capsule (`BODY`) and paw contacts (`uPaws[8]`) that bend the grass only where the cat passes. Measured in the game: paw effect of 0 pixels in the low view and 0.94 % in the top view, imperceptible by eye. A blade recovery time of 0.4 s is a proposal; the frame cost on a real graphics card is unknown.

## Known disagreements between the studies

These differences come from different cats, contexts or definitions, and each study keeps its own value:

- Walk cycle: *Stray* ≈ 0.8 s, real cat 2.04 s (probably slowed or slow).
- Lying down: *Stray* ≈ 1.6 s in all, real cats ≈ 1.5 to 2.5 s.
- Head lead before lying on the flank: ≈ 0.4 s (back study) and ≈ 0.375 to 1.5 s (flank study).
- Stretch forward hold in *Stray*: 2.5-3 s (first study) and ≈ 1.4 s (second study, whole sequence ≈ 2.6 s).
- Walk tail: low in *Stray*, nearly vertical on the real cat filmed; both exist depending on context.
- Eye spacing: 0.544 of the head width (literature) and 0.438 of the outer ear base spacing (photos). The denominators differ and are not comparable.
- Belly during petting: the behaviour review says to avoid it, while the back study uses belly-rub videos.
